# SATB Christmas carols with Muse Choir

**Fifteen Christmas carols**, each with four labelled vocal staves, a saved MuseScore
score, a PDF and retained source material. All use **Muse Choir → Sopranos, Altos,
Tenors and Basses**. The three additions also have MP3 and MusicXML exports.

The briefs are [`PROMPT.md`](PROMPT.md) and
[`PROMPT-02-additional-carols.md`](PROMPT-02-additional-carols.md). The original twelve
scores and PDFs are unchanged. The three additions were completed on 22 November 2026.

### Retained starter files and rights

- **First twelve:** Open Hymnal Project, editor Brian J. Dumont, downloaded 21 November
  2026. Each `source/` retains the unmodified PDF, ABC, MIDI and copying page. All are
  marked public domain in words, music and setting. The project's assessment is based
  on US law. Some encodings cite later hymnals as well as the credited historical
  settings; no independent audit of those later editorial contributions is claimed.
- **Irby / Winchester Old:** the **full, byte-identical 1909 hymnal PDF** is retained
  once in [`once-in-royal-davids-city/source/`](once-in-royal-davids-city/source/hymns-ancient-and-modern-1909.pdf),
  with a relative symlink in the other carol's `source/`. Each has a convenient two-page
  extract whose printed content is unchanged, plus Archive metadata. The complete
  book is about 62 MB; it is kept because the brief requests the unmodified starter.
- **We Three Kings:** the unmodified one-page Mobile Hymns PDF and the site's
  copyright-handling page are retained. Its public-domain marking covers the score
  being transcribed; no claim is made that this is the original 1863 trio voicing.

Each carol's `NOTES.md` distinguishes the composition/text from its setting and edition,
records exact URLs, retrieval date and source page numbers, and describes changes.
There were no purchases, account-based downloads or bypasses of access restrictions.

Install MuseScore Studio 4.x and Muse Choir through the Linux **Muse Sounds Manager**,
or the appropriate installer for your OS. The official
[MuseSounds installation guide](https://handbook.musescore.org/sound-and-playback/musesounds)
and [Mixer handbook](https://handbook.musescore.org/sound-and-playback/mixer) describe
installation and sound selection. The earlier experiment also has
[local reproduction notes](../musescore-choir-experiment/README.md#reproducing-this-on-another-machine).
The library itself is an external dependency and is not committed.

Open a `.mscz`, dismiss any startup notices, and press **F10**. The four Sound cells
should show:

| Staff / instrument ID | Library | Sound preset | Muse UID |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soprano / `soprano` | Muse Choir | Sopranos | 19 |
| Alto / `alto` | Muse Choir | Altos | 20 |
| Tenor / `tenor` | Muse Choir | Tenors | 21 |
| Bass / `bass` | Muse Choir | Basses | 22 |

The active profile is `MuseSounds`. The metronome stays on MS Basic and is muted.
The stored instrument ID and the selected playback sound are checked separately.
If Muse Choir is missing, installing/opening the score alone does not establish that
Muse Sounds is playing: check the actual sound selection and sampler loading.

### What the voices sing

The official [Muse Choir user guide, p. 3](https://production-v2.muse-cdn.com/library-user-guides/2be98874-810b-485a-b238-d7c1eb857372.pdf)
documents **Ahh, Ooh and Mmm** vowel selection through staff text or those special lyric
values. This does **not** imply synthesis of arbitrary English words. No explicit vowel
changes are inserted in these carols.

**Observed here on 4.7.5:** *We Three Kings* rendered with Muse Choir is byte-identical
with and without its ordinary lyric elements, and differs from the MS Basic render.
Two independent MS Basic renders are byte-identical too. See
[`additional-playback-check.json`](evidence/additional-playback-check.json).
Thus these recordings demonstrate vowel-based choir playback; the carol words remain
in the notation. This is narrower than claiming that all lyric text is always ignored.
The earlier experiment's silent-fallback test was not repeated by moving the installed
sampler library; successful sampler loading and profile differences were checked instead.

## How the scores were made

**Original twelve:** Open Hymnal's own ABC, used to typeset its PDFs, was converted with
abc2xml v269 and the existing `build.sh` pipeline. SA/TB became four vocal staves;
Tenor moved to octave-treble clef without changing sounding pitch. Text-only stanzas were
retained, and the scores were GUI-saved to pin the sounds. Read a carol's `NOTES.md` for
its individual source and alterations. `build.sh` still handles these ABC sources and
explicitly skips the PDF transcriptions.

**Three additions:** manually read from the retained PDFs, entered with
[`tools/additional_carols.py`](tools/additional_carols.py), imported into MuseScore with
the existing `tools/carols.mss` style, and GUI-saved. There was **no OMR or MIDI import**.
`source-ledger.tsv` in each new carol folder records all four voices' sounding pitches
and quarter-note durations, counting source bar positions from 1. This is a readable
transcription record, not an independent MIDI source. Every bar was proofread against
the PDF, including irregular phrase bars, unisons, accidentals, slurs and ties.

*Irby* retains its opening musical repeat; the other two have no repeats. The 1909
sources print no time signatures, so 4/4 (*Irby*) and 4/2 (*Winchester Old*) are editorial.
Their short phrase bars remain short without added rests. Their original note values
are retained. All three added tempos are editorial. The per-carol notes describe lyric
layout, omitted redundant melody/chord labels, and the potentially surprising readings.

To reproduce the additions' CLI-stage files **on a working branch**:

```bash
python3 tools/additional_carols.py
for slug in once-in-royal-davids-city while-shepherds-watched we-three-kings; do
  QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen musescore --sound-profile MuseSounds \
    --style tools/carols.mss -o "$slug/$slug.mscz" "$slug/$slug.musicxml"
done
python3 tools/additional_carols.py --append-stanzas
```

Then open and **save each score in the GUI**, confirm its Mixer assignments, and export
the PDF. The builder does not pretend that a CLI save fulfils the GUI requirement.
Render an optional recording with:

```bash
QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen musescore --sound-profile MuseSounds \
  -b 192 -o we-three-kings/we-three-kings.mp3 we-three-kings/we-three-kings.mscz
```

The additions use only Python's standard library for entry/verification; Poppler and
MuseScore must be available. They do not need abc2xml or music21.

## Limitations and source decisions

- The full restart screenshot check covers **one new carol**, as required by the
  additional brief. Separate screenshots for the other fourteen were not added.
  The original twelve's earlier missing restart screenshots remain a historical gap;
  their files are unchanged.
- Playback performs one stanza, including *Irby*'s internal repeat. It does not
  automatically perform every text stanza, and it does not sing the English lyrics.
- Manual PDF readings can contain errors despite the bar-by-bar proofread. The
  committed source, ledger, editable MusicXML and per-carol notes make them reviewable.
  A specialist musical review and human listening remain useful; neither is claimed.
